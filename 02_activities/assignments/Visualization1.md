Link To Dataset - https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/outbreaks-in-toronto-healthcare-institutions/

>What software did you use to create your data visualization?
I utilized Visual Studio code and Python Matplotlib library to build the visualizations.

>Who is your intended audience?
The intended audience for this visualization is public health authorities and healthcare institutions as this shows the prevalence of outbreak incidents in 
City of Toronto in 2024.

>What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization?
I am trying to convey an overview of the infectious outbreaks, that occured in various healthcare institutions in City of Toronto in 2024.

>What design principles (substantive, perceptual, aesthetic) did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply these principles? With what elements of your plots? 
I included the following principles in my visualization:
1. Substantive - I am trying to emphasize on the clarity of information as it is conveyed by the data by keeping it easy to understand with simple charts like the bar charts which is showing the number of outbreak incidents by Outbreak setting and outbreak type.
2. Perceptual - I added two different graphs to clearly display the association between the number of incidents and setting and Outbreak type.
3. Aesthetics - I ensured these are aesthetic with large enough labels/titles/legends. The colors are used to ensure that people belonging to all vision capabilities - normal/red-green colorblind and blue-yellow colorblind  people can decipher. There is also a Alt-text that's added to describe what the chart is trying to convey.

>How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 
I have added the links to datasets and Python codes to make it completely reproducible.

>How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible? 
To make it accessible and easy to understand I have done the following:
1. Added simple bar charts and labels 
2. Added a single basic color for the charts
3. Added a box to state the full-form of the x-axis parameter labels
4. Added Alt-text to keep the cognitive load low

>Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?
The public health authorities and the healthcare institutions that are talked about in the dataset would benefit from this data as it would provide insights on the type of infection and the type of setting that are contributing to most of outbreak incidents. Eventually, they would be able to plan strategies accordingly to stengthen the infection control measures at this settings.

>How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 
I chose the features based on the relevance to what I am trying to convey. As I am trying to convey an overview of the outbreak incidents that occured in healthcare institutions in Toronto in 2024, I chose the outbreak setting, outbreak type features. And, hence did not include features like address, date of onset, type of infection etc.
