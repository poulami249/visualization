      ```
      Your answer...

      The good visualization is a help desk dashboard where it is displaying the number of service requests and their status. Other than displaying the total number of requests, it has also categorized the numbers of requests by their urgency and time of the year. Additionally, there are filters to filter the page with month or urgency level which makes the dashboard dynamic. On the right hand side, the requests are also categorized by ticket status and issue categories. It is also showing the avg number of open requests and customer satisfaction scores. On the bottom we can see the % of open tickets and the number of days they have been open for. We can also see the requests by owner group and their details on bottom right in a scrollable list.

      I categorized this visualization as a good visualization because of the following reasons - it is Aesthetic, Substantive and Perceptual. 
      1. It is aesthetic because it utilizes a good mix of colors to differentiate between the requests based on urgency level. They are not too distracting and helping to differentiate between the categories, making it easy to understand for the audience. 
      
      2. It is substantive because it is correctly presenting the data without leading the audience. The filters are helping to understand the service request load by month or urgency level.
      
      3. And, lastly it is perceptual because the dashboard is easy to decipher and has a clear intent of displaying the various services request tickets and their details. It also uses simple terminologies and does not increase cognitive load for the audience.


      The bad visualization is a Superstore sales dashboard. It is showing the Sales and Profit for every region for each of their departments - Consumer/Corporate/Home Office. The sales and profit also have line graphs to show the numbers by year and quarter. I categorized this as a bad example of data visualization for the following reasons:

      1. The dashboard looks decent but it is not intuitive for audience to understand what each number or chart represents. Additionally, the colors that are segregating departments are distracting as they are reappearing for each region. Also, the labels of the numbers on the right side of line chart represented by red/blue bar is not consistent - for some it is showing sales/profit of 2023/2024 and for some it is showing sales/profit of 2024. There is also no legend for the colors beside the line chart. Also, the same shade of blue color is being used for comsumer and profit and could be confusing. 
      
      2. Even though the dashboard talks about sales/profit of a superstore, it does not specify the total sales/profit numbers and percentages of sales/profits by region. Although, the sales and profit numbers by region, departments, quarter of the year are substantive, it does not provide enough details for the audience to have a clear overview of the sales.

      3. It is somewhat perceptual as it does convey that the dashboard's intent of displaying the sales and profit by region, departments, quarter of the year. However, there are no filters to enable the audience to filter the number by year/region/departments. It also does not specify the country or continent of the region. Also, the segregation of the departments is not clear - Consumer and corporate does not really match with Home-office category, which seems to be a category of product. That leads to another flaw of the dashboard which is not specifying the sales/profits by category of products. Additionally, the line chart beside the sales/profit numbers is very hard to compare for all regions/departments. This could introduce cognitive load on the audience.
      ```
